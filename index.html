from my cousin.

gnu • august 3, 2026

Every July, something significant always happen at least for the last two years.

In 2024, I had started living for a bit with my mentor, forming a bond with Kirby, and the various experiences of going home after 17 months.

In 2025, I had been reunited with Kirbys and taught myself about love and appreciating all things there is. I had written about this experience in the previous prompt cape marguerite from the amazing sunset on my first walk with Kirby, the films I watched, and the release of arguably my most favourite album recently, SWAG by Justin Bieber.

There was only one highlight this year in July: my cousin had finally been rostered a Sydney flight.

I had always dreamed of roaming around Sydney with someone I love - or someone dear to me. I had always wished it to be my mother, but the circumstances for that to happen is not there yet. I had always to find love and connection with someone in the city which had healed and nurtured me so much. I dreamed of walking through gardens, eating in a fancy restaurants, having breakfast along a quaint european style street; and just talking.

Quite frankly, living in paradise and in a land I only used to dream of can only go as far in terms of falling-in love in paradise. Recalling my memories in this city, I felt there was something missing. I only used to enjoy those moments by myself to the point that I have numbed myself to it.

I have a lot of cousins on my mother’s side (I don’t know who and how many on my father’s side) but she had always been the one who was most dear to me. I like thinking that I grew up all alone but I do admit that there are these small and charming memories where I felt like I had an older sister who I play around with and that was her. We played around a lot back then, she would tease me, and we even cuddled together sometimes keeping each other safe. Honestly, I had some of my happiest memories when she visited me and my mother.

Amongst us cousins, we were also the most ambitious. We are the only ones who are currently overseas and trying to build a life. We are also the ones to break our chain generational trauma instilled by our parents & grand-parents (which we did talk about and I’ll outline here.) She also holds herself with such self-respect after all she’s been through with her family and her exes.

These memories, conversations this city, and the conversations we had eventually became the backdrop on what eventually became the highlight of another memorable July.

We met at the lobby of Novotel Sydney Airport where Qatar Airways usually lodge their flight crews. For the first time in almost 18 months, I had finally seen a family member again; and the first time since I got here, I got to spend time with a family member in Sydney. We stayed in room 516. We only had 24 hours so we maximised our time. She got dressed (I still don’t get women and how they take 1-hour just to dress up) and we immediately went off for Circular Quay as I intended for us to eat in a fine dining restaurant by the Sydney Opera House.

It was a nice Winter night just right after sunset as we walked to Wolli Creek train station. We had first talked about me being here and how abrupt it was. She went off for Qatar around October 2022, while I went off for Sydney last February 2023. Hers was expected as she was really working for it for years. Mine wasn’t. Sydney wasn’t even in my radar at the time she left.

She told me how shocked everyone is because it was so sudden. I understood it. Because I had been tied to my mother for so long that everyone assumed I am hyper-dependent to her; that there was no way I would leave her side. During that time, I kept so much to myself that I never told anyone what I was really feeling. I was so fed up with my mother back then. I was sick and tired that everything in my life revolved around her decisions. Granted, I wasn’t chained to her or anything, and I’m grateful for that. I guess ever since the lockdown started, I was robbed of so many opportunities and experiences that I had just had enough. I left.

One thing to take note of is that I usually also don’t open up to anyone - even to my family members, even to her. I don’t know why, but this time; I was willing to let my wall down even just a little bit because like I said; she matters so much to me.

There was one conversation which I was hoping we won’t go to (but we did immediately lol.) We were supposed to meet almost 18-months ago last 2025 of February because I was home and she was also going to be home too at around the second to third week of that month.

I didn’t meet her despite me being home, and she knew. She asked about it right away.

While waiting for a train at Wolli Creek Station, after some brief pictures; we sat down.

She asked me, “Bebe, I have a question but only if you want to lang”

My heart sank a bit because I already know where it’s going to go.

“What happened in Cebu?” she said.

I clinched my lips and sat there silent for a solid minute because I really don’t know what to say.

“It’s okay if you don’t want to.” but I quickly exclaimed “No it’s okay.”

When she went home that month, my mom had told her that I was still in Cebu and she cannot meet me. She said that I owe home some juicy stories then.

I really don’t know what to say or where to start. Maybe you can say that it’s a highly-sensitive topic to me, or I had repressed these memories so much that I have to dig to unravel them again, or maybe I’m not too comfortable just being vulnerable to anyone, ever.

I was silent until the train came as I am at a lost for words.

“Was it official?”

“No.” I said.

“It was no label I guess even it sort of not it.”

“Was it because of you or her?” She asked back.

“I don’t know, me?” (It wasn’t me. I don’t know how to answer this.)

I say this because I somehow was able to convince myself that whatever happened in Cebu wasn’t real—that I was just being used to some degree. I wish I can fully recount to her every detail with such passion, but what is to be passionate when nothing was given back to me?

She acted all giddy because it was the first time. It was the first time she has heard about someone I’m associated with; and of course, as someone who is like a sister to me, really likes to talk about this.

I never met her last ex. They got together when she went off to work in Qatar Airways, and I never got a chance to meet him. But they broke up just earlier this year.

I didn’t find the right words or dig enough through my memories to know what to say to her at that time. So we talked about her ex first.

“What happened? It sucks I never met him.” I asked.

She has a natural talent of recounting every single detail of a story and it’s hard for me to catch-up sometimes (maybe it’s a girl thing.) She also told loads of stories then. I’ll try to recount it here.

I think it started when she had an argument with him. The guy was under a lot of distress and all of that distress was expressed and handed over to her. He started screaming and doing all sorts of stuff.

Her recounting the story made me admire my cousin even more. She stood her ground. She told him that he’s better than this. Moreover, she was willing to stay despite the fact. Perhaps she knew him truly.

Eventually the guy initiated it. His excuse was, “I became someone who I really resented directly to you: my father. So I will leave.” I told her how much I hate that excuse because there’s no accountability and he’s taking the easy way out; he was being a coward. “I agree!” she says.

“If someone is worth it, then they will change for you. He wasn’t willing to do that.”

“Exactly. It’s like I’m being the one to blame.” she replied.

Funnily enough, they kept in-contact after the break-up; mostly just asking what’s up or simple greetings. She never looked for anyone else, neither is the guy post break-up. I asked my cousin: “If you felt that he had taken accountability for himself and is now willing to change for you, would you have came back?”

“Of course.” she said.

Then the more she told the story, the more it affirms how much disgust I have with mostly Filipino men in general.

She heard some gossips from her fellow crew mates (the guy was a cabin crew as well) that there was someone else he’s dating right now. The more she digs, she found out when they became together: shortly after they broke up.

And the gall for that guy to keep in-touch with my cousin? My cousin said she felt so disgusted, and rightfully so. She said that she wouldn’t continue being in-touch with him if she had known there was already someone else. She’s not a home wrecker she said.

I had so much respect for her after this story.

We were nearing Circular Quay Station. After her story, I had some comfort of opening up to her, but I didn’t know where to start.

We walked along Circular Quay with a very nice view of the Opera House and the Harbour Bridge. We arrived at Luna Lu Restaurant just by The Rocks. It’s an Asian Fusion restaurant but I really just wanted to buy her an Opera House-style dessert which I think is a nice welcome to the city.

I don’t know exactly what I said, but I did share some details like how I met her and other stuff.

“You know for us women, if someone travels through literally continent just to see someone is a sign of something serious.” she said.

“Yeah but I don’t know.” I was still at a loss for words. I really want to tell her everything but I don’t know what to say.

Eventually we talked about what’s going on with her right now. Just a month ago, she said a new guy had been following her online. Based on how she described him, he sounded nice. She had a lot of reservations at first considering it was a random stranger in the internet.

She said the conversations eventually became deeper. She said that she liked talking to him.

Until recently: she said she didn’t like how one of their conversation went, and she looked to me for advise. At one time, she said she was at Church and being with God. She asked where the guy was, and he said that he was out drinking with friends.

I asked her, “Was it just friendly drinking, or heavy drinking?”

“Just out with friends.” She called the guy out and sort of made him guilty because of it, even though it was something so trivial.

It irked me as well because it reminded me of her and how I felt I was being scrutinised by her for small trivial stuff rather than me.

“Sounds like a very trivial thing right? I feel like you shouldn’t base your entire relationship on something like this. After all, I don’t see anything wrong it.”

Funnily enough, at this point, I started seeing more of her through my cousin which will make me realise something later on.

I don’t know where to preface my story for my cousin, instead I started with this: “Did you know that I have severe trust issues then even to my mother?”

She asked when it started, but I don’t really know. My mom loved me unconditionally, but at some point she started disappointing me so much that it was so easy for me to leave her and move here.

I told her more stuff even why it took me so long to even try to be in a relationship with someone: that I felt like I was carrying so much weight and toxicity back then that I didn’t want to burden someone else to carry it.

Because I did liked people back then—if I actually tried, I admit I may be in a healthy relationship right now. But as I grew closer to them, the more I get anxious, and the more of myself comes out so I run away because then I taught it was the most selfless thing to do.

There is another thing about guilt: I feel guilty to even be feeling those kinds of stuff towards my mother and my friends. That’s why I kept all those emotions deep within me and never letting them out. I didn’t want to hurt my mother because I know she tries so hard, and I didn’t want to burden my friends with it. In short, my walls were being built and have become so high and tick after 26-years of building it.

She said she understands the guilt became she felt it too. We talked at lengths about breaking the chain of generation traumas. I said I felt guilty of feeling these because I know deep down that my cousins have it worse. Her mother died at an early age leaving her and her sister with my mom’s brother who is honestly hopeless. She also has another half-sister from the same father whose mother also died when she was 15-years-old. Their father used to be a serial gambler who jokes and not take anything seriously. He neglected his children so much. There were even more disgusting moments like when she tried inviting him to meetup with her siblings. He did but at the cost of asking money from my cousin.

My family’s history is a long list of illegitimate children born outside marriage with multiple partners. Our grandfather had already been married, broke up briefly with the woman, then had three children with another women who became our parents. He eventually went back to the initial woman until his death.

Needless to say, the three siblings that are our parents are all neglected. I can only recount short stories from my mom such as when she tries to get the attention of my grandmother who was busy gambling in Mahjong, she basically gave her a coin and go somewhere else. Funnily enough too, all three siblings has had children with multiple partners. All of us cousins have half-siblings in some form. I was probably the most fortunate among all of them as I still am able to get some of my father’s fortune even though he bore me in secret from his wife. I didn’t know what to feel about it then and I felt an immense amount of guilt to even resenting my parents for putting me in this situation because I know that my mom was doing her best, and my dad didn’t abandon me financially.

She sympathised with my feelings and told me that I have a good heart. I guess there is a degree of maturity between us two when talking breaking these patterns. She loves more profoundly her sisters, her nephew, and all those who had helped her growing up including my mom. I affirm my family situation and am handling it peacefully—knowing full-well to do better and not be like my mom nor my dad.

After dinner, we then walked to the Opera House to take some picture. He talked more about the guy she’s talking to. She looked happy, inspired, and more importantly safe. She talked about fearing about “losing herself” when he’s associated with a guy again. I calmly told her, “loving is not about losing oneself, but becoming a fuller version of oneself.” To be so in-love that you are inspired to become a better person, and your partner is able to bring out the best in you; not lock you in their ideal version of you; to love every version of you in the future.

We took some pictures and headed home. She begged me to ask what her name was. “Aleli,” I said after more than a year of not saying that name. She smiled as if looking at me and how much I’ve grown up.

We woke up right away and headed for Surry Hills and ate at the “Four Ate Five” Cafe along Crown Street (because the building’s number is 485.) It’s a cafe Vinnie recommended to me and it did not disappoint. I am also in-love with Surry Hills aesthetic. It’s ladened with deciduous trees that changes every season, and the houses are heritage protected for their classical Victorian-era English architecture. I love this because it reminds me that I am sort of in Europe—which is something I only used to dream about as a kid. It’s also riddled with different bookstores, vinyl shops. The one I most liked was just nearby called the “TITLE Store” which features countless of classic books, vinyls, and a film processing company just right upstairs which I just wandered to the last time I visited.

While eating at Four Ate Five, we talked a little bit more about her because my cousin was very curious to know more about her. Crown St has a very English charm about it and we eventually talked about romcoms, specifically Notting Hill. I know too much about romcoms despite not being a big fan as a kid thanks to my mom and her who will always watch something in the TV and I’ll be “forced” to watch it too. I mentioned, “Her most favourite film is Notting Hill actually.” She screamed and acted all giddy when I mentioned the film. “Really? I love that film too.”

“When I was in London, I went to Notting Hill and there was this bookshop where they shot the film. I bought fridge magnets then for me and your mom.” she says. I know this because I can still recall the fridge magnet that was on our fridge the last time we were home.

She likes flowers too, I said.

“Really! I love flowers.”

“What’s her favourite flower?”

“Daisies.” I replied.

“Mine are Tulips.” and she recounted how much she loved walking through a tulip garden when s he was in Amsterdam.

She eventually said, “Bebe, sayang you should have introduced her to me, I felt like I would’ve understood her as a girl and I like her based on what you told me.”

“Next time, make sure you introduce someone to me ah.” she continued.

For some reason, even if it wasn’t directed at me, I was touched. I agree, I should have. She kept on talking about putting up walls and such, not trusting men, and the conversation we had the night before reminded me so much of her. I wanted to help her so much back then, but I just didn’t know how. Everything felt so distant and I have come to a realisation that even to her, I had my walls up so high that it omitted any form of deep connection which could’ve happened.

We took a bus to Bondi Beach. While we were talking, I have come to a realisation why I liked her in the first place.

She likes to play basketball which is amazing because it’s so unorthodox for a woman. When she was posting about playing basketball I initially thought it was her way of grieving her exes. Her recent ex played basketball. Before that, her ex was also Matthew Aquino—son of a famous Philippine Basketball Association player I think whom she met while he was playing in the National University.

But she told me otherwise. She talked about her dad and though her dad is so imperfect and self-centred, the irony she tells me is that she still loves him so much. She still wants her dad to visit other countries someday. When she was a kid, she was very boyish—she wore boyish clothes, is part of a dance group, and has a very street-style aesthetic. Her dad never had a male child so he sort of moulded her in a boyish fashion. She said he used to take her to his basketball games and how she was inspired by it. One day, when she was playing a game, she recalled how fulfilled she is seeing her father light up at the sight of her scoring a point.

I was very touched. Then suddenly, I realised something.

The reason why I liked her in the first place is I saw so much of the other people whom I loved growing up through her. Her love for romcoms, flowers, and art reminds me so much of my cousins who express their love in those forms. She introduced me to watching Formula 1. It was easy for me to pick-up because my mom and dad was an avid F1 fan, bonded over it, and my dad would usually send F1 gifts to my mom from those he attended. I got into tennis because my parents loved watching it so much too and my mom even named me after a tennis player. I thought to myself then: “You’re so much like my mom and my cousins and I love that so much. Yet, you look so troubled and I want you to flourish so much so I try the best way I can.”

I guess in some way, the love I tried to give for her was the love I would’ve given back to my mom and my cousins. It’s like I knew her my entire life that’s why it was so easy for me to choose her without a second thought—which I would’ve done so if it was any other person.

The tragedy is that I’ve never told her this, and it’s probably too late to do so. Moreover, I unknowingly had my walls up high for her and that’s why I never realised this in-front of her.

On my cousin and the guy she’s talking to, I told her: “Oh so that must be what it feels like to connect.” Funnily enough, I overheard them calling via phone. The guy was so sorry about the whole drinking incident. My cousin laughed, “Omg, I’m so sorry it’s okay. I made you feel so guilty about that.”

I also recounted some details to her about what happened

“Ah so you had walls up for her which is why she felt no connection.”

“Perhaps.”

“Do you wish you could’ve done it differently?”

“Yes, of course.”

“Do you have plans on reaching out to her again?”

“No. I’m sick and tired of being the one always chasing. I had always been the one second-guessing everything.”

“Do you miss her?”

“Maybe. I don’t know.”

We went around Bondi, took a bus to Watsons Bay, then a ferry through the harbour. Some shopping, then she eventually left. I was at a loss for words during the latter half and we really didn’t talk more about it anymore.

Recounting these events, and moreover, talking to someone about it made me reminisce the whole thing once again.

For the past months, I’ve drowned myself in endless work and stress to the point that I am fearing for my health. The past two weeks, I’ve worked (voluntarily) for a straight 7-days a week. It’s a great distraction to be honest which helps in some way repress these memories. There was a point where I know I needed love—chronic stress is a dangerous thing. It lessens your life by about 50%, and everyday I feel like I feel it in every cell in my body. Moreover, I recall the deaths of some famous people I admire who had lived with partners all their life: Queen Elizabeth II and Jimmy Carter; who died just months after their spouses. The widowhood effect is a proves that stress is a killer, and I recognise it’s effects on me.

Yet, I do not see myself ever entertaining another relationship. I feel like I’ve imparted a sentence on myself as a result of my ambition, tenacity, and unwillingness to yield. My walls are built so high for 26-years so much that it omits me from any deep relationship I can have in the future. I also, still, do not see a life for me past 30-years-old.

I do not know where to place her in my life, nor do I feel like she made any significant impact on who I am. The last thing I told her was I didn’t feel “seen” as I started to realise what that world finally meant. I don’t fault her for that because I feel I was too sheltered. I wasn’t enough of a womaniser to make her feel something. But at the same time, she never gave me anything to hold on to. She gave me every reason to not care anymore. But the thing is, I feel like something is keeping me to hold on to these. It’s quite annoying.

To end, there is a white lie to one of the questions my cousin asked me,

I do miss her.
